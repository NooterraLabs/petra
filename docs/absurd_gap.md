A gap count compares with another run's only at the same generator commit and seeds, and only when each seed's stream hash agrees: the hash covers every design text, so it also shows a registry or sampler change that moved the stream.

Each design is a sample from the steel deck's own samplers (`petra/models/steel/generate.py`) or an example, mutated 0 to 3 times along the dimensions that decide whether parts fit, with its load drawn log-uniform (1 to 40 kip, 1 to 200 kip for flange_force), so most strength checks pass and fit is what is tested. The circuit, truss, joint and demand start from their examples and mutate the same way; a joint's pull is drawn log-uniform from 0.05 to 3 times its bolts' rough proof load. Petra checks it (`check_design`, the solvers as evidence), and the outside judge in `judges/` reads it. **Judged** means the judge finds at least one violated condition. **Gap** means judged and Petra PASS: a design Petra certifies that cannot be built as drawn, or is unsafe for a reason Petra's models never name. P1 drives the gap to zero (docs/plan.md §6).

## What the judge did not decide

Per design, the notes the judge left (counted by kind and the start of the note):

- demand: the judge's own bound at the stated capacity (185)
- joint: the judge's own linear bolt force (1047)
- truss: the judge's own collapse factor without self-weight (578)

By construction, for every design:

- flange_force: no beam or connecting part is drawn, and `distance` does not say whether it reaches the force's centre or its near edge, so where the bearing sits on the member is not decided.
- stiffened_seat: the seat plate's width across the support is not drawn; only the stiffener's fit is judged.
- seated: the top angle's position and length are not drawn; only the seat's fit is judged.
- Every support is read as a column. Under a girder reading, a beam framing to the web would also need its depth to fit between the girder's flanges or a cope; the grammar does not say which, so that condition is not judged.
- Bolt holes are taken as standard (Table J3.3), the smallest the grammar allows, and fits are physical (a hole wholly on the part), not Table J3.4's minimum edge distances, which Petra checks where it names them.
- `steel.beam_side.fillet` and `steel.support_side.web_fillet` flag a part that clears the flanges but sits on the web-to-flange fillets, inside kdes (the shapes table's detailing value). A fabricator can clip such a corner, so these are counted apart from parts that hit solid steel or leave the member.
- Where the lines leave a position open, the judge takes the most favourable one: a symmetric pair of bolt lines is centred on the column, a one-sided group (a single angle, a bracket's columns) goes wherever it fits, and a beam end and its cope reach as far out as the lines allow.
- Wrench clearance, weld access and bolt length are not judged; Petra names clearance for some plies.
- The truss judge cannot decide out-of-plane bracing, which the truss model assumes and never states.
- The circuit judge reads the terminal's conductor range from the registry, whose rows are tagged judged.
- A joint's lines draw no bolt length, hole, edge distance or washer, so grip, edge and pull-through fit are not judged. Every generated joint waives plate bearing, which Petra does not model, so its tension check decides.
- A demand's lines draw no part. The judge takes its capacity against its own tests only.
