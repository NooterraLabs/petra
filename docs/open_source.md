# Running Petra in the open

How projects like Petra are run as open source, and what Petra should copy. Petra is a checker whose verdicts people are asked to trust, so the closest precedents are proof assistants (Lean, Rocq, Isabelle), verified systems (seL4, CompCert), qualified toolchains (Ferrocene, AdaCore), open engineering solvers (code_aster, OpenFOAM, CalculiX, MOOSE) and open benchmarks (SWE-bench, PutnamBench, Terminal-Bench).

Three research passes read the sources on 2026-11-06. I re-checked the claims the playbook leans on: the Kernel Arena, Amazon's Lean donation, NASA's DO-330 study, the SWE-bench Verified retirement, Astral's acquisition, the MCP donation and seL4's move to a Swiss association. "Unconfirmed" marks a claim no pass could source.

## 1. What the comparable projects do

### Trust is earned by independent re-checking and a public bug record

- **Lean publishes a ladder of checks and the assumptions left at each rung.** At the top, comparator rebuilds a proof in a sandbox and re-checks it with the official kernel and with nanoda, an independent kernel in Rust. The guide reserves that rung for high-risk sources such as unaligned AI ([Validating proofs](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/ValidatingProofs/)).
- **Lean runs a public arena of independent kernels.** Anyone may submit a checker or a test. Each checker is scored on every test, including mathlib, and trivial checkers such as `always-reject` sit on the board as controls ([Kernel Arena](https://arena.lean-lang.org)). It paid off. Lean 4.32.2 fixed a soundness bug that got through comparator, and nanoda rejected the bad proof because it does arithmetic differently ([release notes](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/releases/v4.32.2/), [de Moura](https://leodemoura.github.io/blog/2026-3-16-who-watches-the-provers/)).
- **AI labs now attack the kernels.** Lean 4.34.0 fixed three bugs that Daniel Selsam of OpenAI found by AI adversarial testing ([release notes](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/4.34.0/releases/v4.34.0/)). The roadmap plans a `--paranoid` mode that checks with every shipped kernel ([roadmap](https://lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1/)).
- **Rocq keeps a templated file of every critical bug since 8.0.** Each entry names the component, affected versions, reporter, exploit and risk, about 97 entries in all ([critical-bugs.md](https://github.com/rocq-prover/rocq/blob/master/dev/doc/critical-bugs.md)). The MetaRocq authors report about one critical bug a year ([paper](https://rocq-prover.org/papers/correct-and-complete-type-checking-and-certified-erasure-for-coq-in-coq)).
- **seL4 lists what its proofs still trust.** The list covers assembly, boot code, the machine interface, hardware and DMA ([Verification](https://sel4.systems/Verification/)).
- **Fuzzing finds bugs where nothing is verified.** Csmith found wrong-code bugs in CompCert only in its unverified front end, and about six CPU-years found none in the verified middle end ([Yang et al.](https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/~regehr/papers/pldi11-preprint.pdf)). Z3 has no small kernel, and a year of fuzzing found 184 soundness bugs in Z3 and CVC4 ([Winterer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08799v4)).

### Regulated users qualify a small checker and rerun the evidence themselves

- **NASA Langley studied qualifying a small proof-certificate checker under DO-330 instead of the model checker that produces the proofs** ([case studies](https://shemesh.larc.nasa.gov/fm/FMinCert/DO-330-case-studies-RC.html)). That is Petra's split between a small kernel and a large untrusted front end.
- **Ferrocene publishes its whole qualification dossier.** The dossier holds the evaluation report, the qualification plan, a matrix from the language specification to the tests, and the assessor's report ([public docs](https://public-docs.ferrocene.dev/main/index.html)). It took no outside contributions at first, so that every change met the qualification rules ([announcement](https://ferrous-systems.com/blog/ferrocene-open-source/)). Its sources disagree on the IEC 61508 level, SIL 4 in the README and SIL 3 in a release post.
- **Qualification is per project.** Tool vendors say a kit never prequalifies a tool, so users rerun the suite in their own setting ([MathWorks](https://www.mathworks.com/products/do-178.html)).
- **Open engineering solvers publish their verification.** MOOSE follows NQA-1 and publishes its quality plan, requirements traceability matrix and V&V report ([MOOSE SQA](https://mooseframework.inl.gov/sqa/)). code_aster lists 4,600 verification tests, splits its manuals into user, theory, validation and administration volumes, runs every test on every change, and issues a stable version every two years ([code_aster](https://code-aster.org/en), [EDF](https://cmm2017.sciencesconf.org/128845/JRL_cmm2017.pdf)).

### Governance keeps merge rights small and control spread out

- **mathlib lets anyone review, but only 28 invited maintainers on two-year terms merge**, through the bors queue ([maintainers](https://leanprover-community.github.io/teams/maintainers.html), [review](https://mathlib.org/contribute/pr-review.html)). AI-assisted pull requests must carry an `LLM-generated` label ([contributing](https://mathlib.org/contribute/index.html)).
- **Lean core takes changes only from the FRO and a small group, and each starts as an issue or RFC** ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)).
- **Proposals need a sponsor, a prototype and a conformance test.** MCP's enhancement proposals reach Final only with a conformance test ([SEP guidelines](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/community/sep-guidelines)). Rust RFCs pass a ten-day final comment period ([rfcs](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs)).
- **Seats are capped per employer.** Python's council allows at most two members from one employer ([PEP 13](https://peps.python.org/pep-0013/)). FEniCS allows at most four seats per organisation, and money does not buy seats ([governance](https://github.com/FEniCS/governance/blob/master/governance.md)).
- **MCP still has named lead maintainers with a veto, but the seats belong to people, and decisions are recorded in public** ([governance](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/community/governance)). Anthropic later gave MCP to a Linux Foundation body ([announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-the-model-context-protocol-and-establishing-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation)).
- **The Isabelle AFP is run like a journal.** Editors referee entries, authors maintain their own, and `sorry` is banned ([about](https://www.isa-afp.org/about/), [submission](https://www.isa-afp.org/submission/)).

### Licence, contributions and the name

- **Lean and mathlib are Apache-2.0** ([lean4](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4), [mathlib4](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4)). MCP uses Apache-2.0 for code and specification and CC-BY-4.0 for docs ([governance](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/community/governance)). Apache-2.0 carries a patent grant that ends for anyone who sues over the work ([licence](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0)).
- **Every switch to a source-available licence produced a funded fork.** HashiCorp's move to BSL produced OpenTofu ([manifesto](https://opentofu.org/manifesto/)). Redis's move produced Valkey ([Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-launches-open-source-valkey-community)). Elastic's produced OpenSearch. Elastic and Redis later added AGPL, and Redis's CEO wrote that the switch hurt its community ([Redis](https://redis.io/blog/agplv3/)).
- **A CLA is what makes a later relicence possible.** The DCO only certifies the right to submit under the project's licence ([DCO](https://developercertificate.org/)). GitLab moved from a CLA to the DCO ([GitLab](https://about.gitlab.com/blog/gitlab-switches-to-dco-license/)). Pydantic and Astral published pledges that their libraries stay permissively licensed ([Pydantic](https://pydantic.dev/articles/company-announcement), [Astral](https://astral.sh/blog/introducing-pyx)).
- **The name is the part that stays protected.** The PSF lets anyone say "compatible with Python" without asking ([PSF](https://www.python.org/psf/trademarks/)). Rust's 2023 draft trademark policy drew complaints, and the adopted policy allows "Rust" in crate names ([devclass](https://www.devclass.com/development/2023/04/11/dont-call-it-rust-community-complains-about-draft-trademark-policy-restricting-use-of-word-marks/1628235), [policy](https://rustfoundation.org/policy/rust-trademark-policy/)). The OpenFOAM Foundation holds all copyright so the code cannot be made private ([IP history](https://openfoam.org/ip-history/)).

### Day-to-day operation

- **Deprecations carry dates.** mathlib marks each deprecated name with a `since` date and may delete it after six months ([style](https://mathlib.org/contribute/style.html)). MCP keeps deprecated features at least twelve months ([versioning](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/versioning)). SemVer reserves 0.y for initial development ([SemVer](https://semver.org/)).
- **Downstream work is built in core CI.** Rocq builds external libraries on every change, and a change that breaks one is fixed or documented ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/rocq-prover/rocq/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)). mathlib keeps a nightly branch against new Lean versions and posts a weekly debt report ([mathlib paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21593v2)).
- **Supply chain.** GitHub's private vulnerability reporting takes reports off the public tracker ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/security-advisories/working-with-repository-security-advisories/configuring-private-vulnerability-reporting-for-a-repository)). PyPI trusted publishing replaces stored tokens with short-lived OIDC tokens ([PyPI](https://docs.pypi.org/trusted-publishers/)), and the standard action uploads Sigstore attestations ([Sigstore](https://blog.sigstore.dev/pypi-attestations-ga/)). OpenSSF Scorecard scores all of this ([Scorecard](https://scorecard.dev/)).
- **Automated bug reports need a rule.** Z3 may close fuzz reports that give only a reproducer ([z3](https://github.com/Z3Prover/z3)).

### Benchmarks and graders

- **Public tasks leak and rot.** OpenAI stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified on 2026-02-23. In an audited subset at least 59.4% of problems had flawed tests, and every frontier model tested could reproduce the reference fixes ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/)). An audit of miniF2F found its formal and informal statements disagree in over half the problems ([audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03108)).
- **What the survivors do.** SWE-bench Pro keeps a private held-out split ([Scale](https://scale.com/blog/swe-bench-pro)). Terminal-Bench embeds a canary GUID with a do-not-train notice ([tbench.ai](https://www.tbench.ai/)). PutnamBench asks every result to name the benchmark commit ([leaderboard](https://trishullab.github.io/PutnamBench/leaderboard.html)). SWE-bench gives a "verified" mark only after maintainers rerun a random subset ([experiments](https://github.com/SWE-bench/experiments)). Lean Eval accepts only solutions that pass comparator ([Lean Eval](https://lean-lang.org/eval/)).

### What kept them alive

- **One funder is the main way these projects nearly die.** CSIRO dropped seL4's team in 2021, a "near-death experience" survived when UNSW stepped in and membership revenue rose about tenfold in two months ([linux.conf.au](https://linux.conf.au/schedule/presentation/59/)). OpenROAD's DARPA funding ended in 2023 ([UCSD](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/open-source-semiconductor-chip-design-tool-celebrates-success)). OpenFOAM's donations are about half their target and falling ([OpenFOAM](https://openfoam.org/news/funding-2025/)). CalculiX rests on two people ([Dhondt](https://www.dhondt.de/20260722_WCCM26_CalculiX.pdf)).
- **Open parts outlive their company.** When Efabless shut down in 2025, its open design flow continued as LibreLane, and the proprietary parts were lost ([FOSSi](https://fossi.foundation/blog/2025-08-17-librelane)).
- **Labs fund neutral checkers because auditors need independence.** Amazon says it backs Lean through the independent FRO so that customers, auditors and regulators can check the proof tools themselves ([Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/news/amazon-is-investing-in-the-lean-focused-research-organization)). The FRO's other funders include Alex Gerko, Harmonic and several foundations ([FRO](https://lean-lang.org/fro/about/)). The Mathlib Initiative pays reviewers and targets 90% of review rounds answered within a week ([Mathlib Initiative](https://mathlib-initiative.org/)).
- **Company-led projects get acquired.** OpenAI agreed to buy Astral on 2026-03-19, and commentators named the permissive licence as users' way out ([Astral](https://astral.sh/blog/openai), [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/19/openai-acquiring-astral/)).

## 2. The playbook for Petra

"Has" is what the repo holds on 2026-11-06. "When" ties each step to `docs/plan.md`.

| Practice | Precedent | Petra has | Petra adds | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Publish what is trusted, and a ladder of checks | Lean, seL4 | `docs/design.md` splits the trusted kernel, the trusted code outside it and the untrusted code | A public page that names each rung (kernel, certificate re-check, twin elaborator, outside judge, CalculiX, Lean port) and prints which rung each verdict reached | Before first publish |
| A templated log of every false PASS, treated as a security advisory | Rocq, Lean | The false PASSes are listed in `docs/plan.md` §1 | `docs/soundness.md` with component, versions, finder, reproducing canary, fix and risk per entry. `SECURITY.md` and private vulnerability reporting | Before first publish |
| Adversarial reports arrive as canaries | Lean, Z3 | `judges/canaries/` and the recall harness | A rule that a soundness report needs a `.petra` file that reproduces it, and that each accepted one becomes a canary | Before first publish |
| Independent checkers, scored in public | Kernel Arena | One kernel, a twin elaborator per deck, the outside judge | A written certificate format and a public corpus of valid and planted-invalid certificates. Any checker, `always-accept` and `always-reject` included, is scored on it | P3 specifications, P7 Lean port |
| Qualify the small checker, publish the evidence | NASA, Ferrocene, MOOSE, code_aster | A kernel of about 1,500 lines, `docs/steel_traceability.md`, the gate logs | An evidence set per release: the traceability matrix, the gate, recall and gap reports, and the rule text each mode cites | Each release |
| Rerun everything on every change | code_aster, Rocq | The full suite, the steel gate, canary recall and the absurd-design gap | CI that runs the gate and recall on every merge and rebuilds every example | Before first publish |
| Apache-2.0, docs CC-BY-4.0 | Lean, MCP | `LICENSE` and `pyproject.toml` say Apache-2.0 | CC-BY-4.0 for `docs/`. The data-licence audit for the shapes table stays as `docs/plan.md` §8 item 3 says | Before first publish |
| DCO and a no-relicence pledge | GitLab, Pydantic, Astral | Nothing | A DCO check on pull requests, and one README sentence saying Petra stays Apache-2.0 | Before first publish |
| A name policy written before forks exist | PSF, Rust, OpenFOAM | Nothing | "Compatible with Petra" free to all. "Petra-checked" only for a verdict from a checker that passes the conformance corpus. Drafted in public before adoption | Before first publish |
| Small merge group, open review, labelled AI work | mathlib, Lean core, Ferrocene | Lanes merge through one integrator | Anyone reviews, a few named maintainers merge, CI gates the merge. Changes to the kernel, registries and modes start as a proposal. AI-assisted pull requests carry a label | Before first publish |
| Proposals need a sponsor, a prototype and a conformance test | MCP, Rust | Registry errata record date, old value and new value | Petra proposals for the language, certificate format, modes and registries. Final status requires a canary or conformance case | When outside contributors arrive |
| Dated deprecation | mathlib, MCP, SemVer | Registry versions count errata | Stay on 0.y until the certificate format is stable. Treat `.petra` syntax and the certificate format as public API, each deprecation dated, kept twelve months after 1.0 | From first publish |
| Benchmark held out, pinned and rerun | SWE-bench Pro, Terminal-Bench, PutnamBench, Lean Eval | P3's plan already has held-out variants from the generator | A canary GUID in every task file, every result pinned to a Petra commit and a bench commit with its logs, maintainers rerunning entries before a mark, and an audit of each task statement against its intent | P3 |
| Supply chain | PyPI, Sigstore, OpenSSF | Nothing published yet | Trusted publishing with attestations, branch protection, Scorecard | At the PyPI claim |
| Many backers, nothing closed that Petra needs to run | seL4, Efabless, Amazon on Lean | Everything is in one open repo | Keep every piece needed to check a design open. Write down when Petra moves to a neutral body, for example once a second organisation pays for full-time work on it, with seats capped per organisation | When a second organisation joins |

## 3. Mistakes to avoid

1. **Relicensing after adoption.** HashiCorp, Redis and Elastic each got a fork backed by the companies they targeted.
2. **Calling anything other than an OSI licence "open source".** The OSI said so of MongoDB's SSPL ([OSI](https://opensource.org/blog/the-sspl-is-not-an-open-source-license)).
3. **A benchmark with no held-out split and no task audit.** SWE-bench Verified and miniF2F both decayed this way.
4. **A trademark policy imposed without consultation.** Rust's 2023 draft is the example.
5. **One funder or one host.** seL4 in 2021 and OpenROAD in 2023.
6. **Hiding soundness bugs.** Lean and Rocq gained trust by publishing every one with its reproducer. A checker's record of found and fixed bugs is part of its evidence.
