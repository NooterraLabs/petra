# twin

A clean-room second implementation of the strength checks for steel connections to AISC 360-22.
It reads Petra design cards and reports, for each connection, the limit states it checks with their
clauses, available strengths and utilisations. Where it disagrees with Petra, the disagreement is
evidence about one of the two.

## What it shares with Petra and what it does not

The twin shares two things with Petra.

- The input format. It reads the same `.petra` cards. Its parser was written from the language
  reference, the steel glossary and the example cards in `examples/steel/`.
- The model family that wrote it. A Claude model wrote the twin, as Claude models wrote Petra. Errors
  of reading that the family makes in common can survive in both.

It shares nothing else.

- No code. The twin never imports `petra` or `judges`, and a test checks that.
- No registry. The twin decides which limit states each kind carries from the Specification and the
  Companion's design examples, and records each decision with its clause below.
- No representation. Quantities are Python `Fraction`s in inches, kips and ksi. Checks are plain
  records of name, mode, clause, nominal strength, factor and demand.

The author did not read Petra's source, tests, judges, scripts or its documents on how it computes a
check, and did not run Petra. The sources read were the Specification (AISC 360-22), the Companion's
design examples (AISC Design Examples v16.0, file P901-23W), the Companion's design tables (P902-23W),
and AISC's shape database (SHAPE160.xlsx).

A second revision fixed points a comparison raised, each stated against the Specification or the
Companion, and checked each against those documents first. Its author followed the same rule. It read
`twin/`, `docs/reference.md`, `docs/steel_glossary.md`, `examples/steel/`, the Specification, the
Companion's design examples and the front matter of its design tables. It did not read Petra's code,
tests, judges, scripts or other documents, and did not run Petra.

A third revision fixed negative strengths that a metamorphic test found where a bolt hole runs past an
edge, on two steel gate designs now in `tests/cards/gate_*.petra`. Its author followed the same rule.
It read `twin/`, `docs/reference.md`, `docs/steel_glossary.md`, the Specification's J3.3 to J3.6,
J3.11 and J4 with the Commentary on J3.5, and the Companion's design examples, and it ran the twin
over `examples/steel/`. Readings 28 to 31 record its decisions.

A fourth revision fixed nine bracket designs that a comparison over generated designs raised, now in
`tests/cards/gate_1_*_bracket.petra`. On six weld brackets the twin read the weld lines by the elastic
method, and on three bolt brackets it gave every bolt the least clear distance in any direction. Its
author followed the same rule. It read `twin/`, `docs/reference.md`, `docs/steel_glossary.md`,
`examples/steel/`, the nine cards, the Specification, the Companion's design examples and its design
tables, each read in place and none copied. It did not read Petra's code, tests, judges, scripts or
other documents, and did not run Petra. It rewrote readings 5 and 24, and readings 32 to 34 record its
other decisions.

## Use

```
py -3.11 -m twin CARD.petra [CARD.petra ...]
py -3.11 -m unittest discover -s twin -t .
```

The first prints one JSON report per card. Each steel component gets a status of `checked`,
`unsupported` (a kind the twin does not model, or too many intervals) or `input_error` (a line it
needs is missing or malformed, or the card puts a bolt line on or past its element's edge). A checked
component lists its checks, the governing one, notes, and the parts of the card the twin does not
model. The governing check has the highest utilisation, except that a detailing limit, which does not
grow with the load, governs only when it fails.

The demand is the largest magnitude over every load range on the component, for the field the kind
carries (`shear`, or `tension` for a hanger). The basis line chooses LRFD (phi) or ASD (Omega).

A length may be an interval, a signed bound `in [lo, hi] ...` or `param NAME`. The twin runs the
component at both ends of every interval and reports each check where its utilisation is highest,
with a note naming those ends. Lines that read one param share its end. The report lists the
intervals. A component with more than 8 intervals is reported as `unsupported`. A load given as
`param NAME` is not read.

## Limit states by kind

Every kind runs its checks against one demand. Bolt lines carry the same four checks wherever they
appear. These are bolt shear (J3.7, Eq. J3-1), bearing and tearout of each ply (J3.11a, Eqs. J3-6a to
J3-6f), the sum of each bolt's least strength (the J3.7 User Note), and slip (J3.9, Eq. J3-4) when the
joint is slip-critical. An eccentric bolt line scales all four by C/n from the IC method of Manual
Part 7. A bracket's bolt group also takes each bolt's tearout along that bolt's own force about the
instantaneous center (reading 24). Where a ply has an edge on the card, the line also checks that
edge's distance along the force against J3.5 (reading 29).

Every block shear check (J4.3) comes in two parts. One is the block through the whole bolt column.
The other is the weakest block through fewer rows, the ones nearest the edge the bolts push toward,
against those rows' share of the demand. Each also checks its edge distance across the force against
J3.5.

## Not modeled

The report lists each of these under `unsupported` when a card asks for it.

- The kinds `stiffened_seat` and `flange_force`, and every kind outside the nine above.
- Any load field other than the kind's own. Axial load on a shear connection is the common case.
- Extended or multi-column single plates.
- Welded support sides of double angles, single angles and tees, and welded seats.
- Slip-critical hangers (the J3.10 tension reduction of slip).
- Block shear of a bolted bracket plate. The eccentric group loads each block with a force and a
  moment, and the documents read give no J4.3 demand for that. Example II.A-22 does not check it.
- Coped sections with c/d or c/hc above 1.0, or with lambda above 2 lambda_p. The documents read
  do not give Manual Eqs. 9-12, 9-13, 9-19b or 9-20b.
- Fit and geometry checks, except J3.5's least edge distance at the edges reading 29 names. Bolt
  spacing (J3.4), the maxima of J3.6, and the edges of seats, hangers and supports are not checked.

## Readings of the documents

Each item says how the twin reads a point the documents leave open, and how sure the reading is.

1. Shear tab eccentricity. The twin takes e = a/2 for every number of rows, as II.A-17A applies
   Manual Table 10-9 (inferred).
2. Single angle support leg. The twin runs the IC method at e = gauge + tw/2. II.A-28A instead takes
   Table 10-11 Case I as a conservative bound for e below 2-1/2 in. The IC method at e = 2.5 gives
   C = 3.065 against the table's 3.07 (measured), so the table is the IC method at 2-1/2 in. (inferred).
   The twin is less conservative here by design.
3. Beam web tearout. The bolts push the web up. J3.11 measures lc in that direction to the next
   hole or the edge of the material, so the top bolt's lc runs to the cope's cut or, uncoped, to the
   beam's top less half the hole. A card without `top` leaves the uncoped top bolt an interior bolt,
   as II.A-1A treats it, which is conservative. A support ply has no edge on the card and keeps every
   bolt interior.
4. Bracket flexural rupture. II.A-22 takes Znet from Manual Table 15-2, which assumes lev = 1-1/2 in.
   The twin uses the plate's actual depth. For II.A-22 its rupture strength is 1,396 kip-in. against
   the example's 1,050 (measured).
5. Welded bracket without returns. The load lies in the plate's plane at e from the support's face,
   normal to the faying surface. In that plane the fillets on the plate's two faces lie on one line,
   so the twin runs the Part 8 IC method on one line of length l loaded at e and multiplies by the
   number of fillets. That is Manual Table 8-4 at k = 0, which Example II.A-2A applies to welds B, a
   fillet line on each angle's outstanding leg with the load 4 in. out from the support. II.A-2A's
   part A reads the same strength from Table 10-2, so Tables 10-2 and 10-3 read welds B this way too
   (inferred). The twin gives the welds B strengths of II.A-2A, II.A-3 and II.A-31 within 0.51
   percent, and C = 3.71 and 4.37 at a = 0 within 0.06 percent (measured). Manual Part 8's own text
   on eccentricity normal to the faying surface is not among the documents read. Table 10-8's
   stiffened seat welds, a case with the same loading, follow the elastic method instead. At L =
   15 in. and W = 7 in. its 139 and 93.0 kips come out at 139.4 and 92.95 by the elastic method with
   0.2L returns and e = 0.8W, and at 156 LRFD by the IC line without returns (measured). The twin
   follows Table 8-4 for the bracket because the Companion applies it to the same line with no
   returns (inferred). The first revision took the elastic resultant with kds = 1.0 (guess). On the
   six weld brackets in `tests/cards/gate_1_*` that read the weld at 1.655 to 1.937, and it now reads
   0.914 to 0.952 (measured). On `examples/steel/weld_bracket.petra` the weld's nominal strength went
   from 70.4 to 137.7 kips (measured).
6. Seat flexure. The twin places the critical section t + 3/8 in. from the angle's back and the
   reaction at the setback plus 1/4 in. plus half the required bearing length. That reproduces Table
   10-5's 113 and 74.9 kips at lb,req = 1-1/8 in. (measured).
7. Steel grades. The A36 values (Fy 36 ksi, Fu 58 ksi) come from ASTM A36, not from the documents read.
8. Hanger stem. The twin yields the stem over the tee's full length. II.D-1 uses a Whitmore width set
   by the welded member, which the card does not describe. The twin is less conservative there
   (measured, 121.5 against 104 kips LRFD for II.D-1).
9. Hanger prying. The twin computes the available bolt tension with prying, Q times B per row, by
   Manual Eqs. 9-23 to 9-30. II.D-1 checks a minimum thickness instead. At that thickness the twin's
   model gives B Q = 15.10 kips per bolt against the required 15.0 (measured).
10. Support base metal at a shear tab weld. The twin checks J4.2(b) on the support's thickness per
    weld line, the form of Manual Eq. 9-6 (inferred).
11. End plate. The twin checks the beam web in shear yielding and rupture over the weld length
    (inferred from the minimum web thickness II.A-11A states).
12. Coped lever arm. The twin takes e = c + setback with the setback as the card states it, as II.A-4
    and II.A-28A do. A coped card without a setback gets no coped flexure check.
13. Coped web shear rupture. For a bolted top cope it does not apply, as II.A-4 states. For a bolted
    double cope the twin checks the net section (inferred). For a welded web it checks hc tw (II.A-6).
14. Coped branch for lambda at most lambda_p. The twin takes Mn = Mp, where Eq. 9-11 meets Mp
    (inferred). Branches the documents do not show are reported as unsupported.
15. Double cope Cb. Manual Eq. 9-21 groups as [3 + ln(Lb/d)](1 - dct/d), at least 1.84. That gives
    II.A-7's printed 1.94 (measured, 1.9365). The first revision read it as 3 + ln(Lb/d)(1 - dct/d),
    which gives 2.59 and overstates Mn wherever lateral-torsional buckling governs. Mp governs II.A-7
    either way.
16. II.A-28A edge distance. The example's bolt check takes the top bolt 1-1/4 in. below the cope and
    its block shear takes 1-1/2 in., as given. The card carries 1-1/2 in. and a test covers both.
17. Underrun. Beam web Leh loses 1/4 in. and a seat's setback gains 1/4 in., as II.A-4 and II.A-12A
    do. The steel glossary states the same rule, so this is a reading the twin may share with Petra.
18. Seat bearing length. The twin bears the beam over the outstanding leg less the setback and the
    1/4 in. underrun, the 3.25 in. II.A-12A uses for its J10.3 branch (measured).
19. Tee stem flexure. The twin takes M = V a with a the bolt line's distance from the flange face
    (inferred from II.A-30).
20. Outstanding angle legs. The twin takes the block's Leh as the outstanding leg less (g - tw)/2,
    with g the support gauge (inferred).
21. Demand. The twin ignores the sign of a load and takes the largest magnitude (inferred from the
    card format, where ranges can be signed).
22. Partial block shear. J4.3 holds on every failure path. A block through the k rows nearest the
    edge carries k/n of the demand, the equal share per bolt the whole-column block also assumes
    (inferred). On the cards in `tests/cards/` and `examples/steel/` no partial path governs. On
    II.A-1A, II.A-28A and II.A-30 it comes within 0.25 percent of the whole column (measured, LRFD).
23. Eccentric bolt groups. The twin keeps C/n times the sum of the bolts' least strengths. II.A-17A
    and II.A-30 apply exactly that to a shear tab and a tee stem. II.A-28A and II.A-19B take C times
    the least single-bolt strength, Manual Eq. 7-15, and II.A-28A calls that conservative. With each
    bolt's own strength in the IC method, C/n times the sum overstates the group by 1.2 percent for
    II.A-17A and by 13 percent for an edge bolt at half strength with e = 6 in. (guess, from a model
    the documents do not give).
24. Bracket holes. J3.11a measures lc in the direction of the force, and in the Part 7 IC method
    each bolt's force lies perpendicular to its radius from the instantaneous center. So each bolt's
    lc runs from the edge of its hole along its own force to the first edge of the plate or of another
    hole (inferred). The plate's edges are Lev above the top row and below the bottom row, Leh behind
    the column farthest from the load, and on a shaped bracket the free edge, which rises from the
    bottom of section A-A to b beyond it at the top (inferred from Manual Fig. 15-2 and the card's
    `free_edge`). The plate is open toward the load. A support ply has no edge on the card, so its
    bolts, pushed the other way, meet only other holes. The group takes C/n times the sum of each
    bolt's least strength, as reading 23 does. C times the least single bolt, Manual Eq. 7-15, would
    read gate-1-08126's group at 0.945 instead of 0.854 and leave the other two bolt brackets as they
    are, since bolt shear is each of their bolts' least strength (measured). The bolt forces about the
    IC run from near vertical to near horizontal, so a long slot either way still gets J3.11a(2) for
    every bolt and a short slot gets J3.9(b) for slip (inferred). The first revision gave every bolt
    the least clear distance in any direction, as II.A-22 takes its least edge distance. On
    gate-1-07378, 08126 and 08786 the plate's tearout then governed at 1.046, 1.013 and 1.099. It now
    reads 0.597, 0.640 and 0.709, and bolt shear governs 07378 and 08786 at 0.959 and 0.964 and the
    group's effective strength governs 08126 at 0.854 (measured). On
    `examples/steel/bolt_bracket.petra` the plate's nominal strength rose from 105.2 to 160.2 kips and
    bolt shear still governs (measured).
25. Bracket slip. The twin takes C times one bolt's J3.9 slip resistance on one slip plane, as it
    scales an eccentric bolt line's slip (inferred). No example in the documents read checks an
    eccentric slip-critical group.
26. Hanger prying under shear. With shear on the bolts, Tc in Manual Eqs. 9-30 and 9-40 is the
    available tension from F'nt of J3.8, as II.A-1B does.
27. Interval lengths. The twin checks only the ends of each interval. A check whose worst case lies
    strictly inside an interval would be missed, and the twin does not prove that none does.
28. Holes that overrun an edge. J3.11a's lc and the net areas of J4.2(b) and J4.3 measure the steel
    between a hole and the next hole or edge. Where a hole runs past the edge or into the next hole,
    the twin counts that span as zero, so no tearout or net area is negative (inferred, since a clear
    distance or an area cannot be negative). Earlier revisions subtracted the overrun. On
    gate-1-04213, whose top slot runs 1/16 in. past the cope's cut, its bolt group read 5.693 kips
    ASD, and 5.595 kips with a web 1/16 in. thicker (measured). It now reads 6.082 kips at both
    (measured). On the Companion cards in `tests/cards/` and on every card in `examples/steel/`, no
    strength changed (measured).
29. Least edge distance. Below Table J3.4's distance, the table's note [a] lets J3.11 and J4 govern
    down to one bolt diameter, and J3.5 adds Table J3.5's C2 for an oversized or slotted hole.
    Example II.A-30 accepts a tee flange edge below the table on that note. The twin checks d + C2 at
    the edges its J3.11 and J4 checks read in the shear connections and the bracket. These are each
    bolt line ply's edge along the force, each block shear check's edge across the force, a bracket
    plate's two edges, and the bottom cope of a double-coped web. Each is taken at the value its
    strength check uses, so the beam web's Leh is less the underrun. The check has mode `edge_min`
    and no phi or Omega. That C2 adds to the note's floor of d, and not only to the table's value,
    is inferred. The check does not grow with the load, so it governs a report only when it fails.
    gate-1-04213's top slot centres 0.5 in. from the cut against d + C2 = 1 in., a utilisation of 2
    (measured).
30. A bolt line on or past its element's edge. The card then puts no hole in that element, so the
    twin reports `input_error` rather than check a load path that is not there.
31. Single angle support gauge. `docs/reference.md` measures `support_gauge` from the angle's heel,
    at the beam web's face, to the bolt line in the outstanding leg, and the II.A-28A card follows
    it. The twin reads it so. gate-1-03786 gives 5.5 in. on a 3.5 in. outstanding leg, which puts the
    line 2 in. past the toe, so the card is refused under reading 30.
32. Weld bracket base metal. Table J2.5 leaves a fillet weld's base metal to J4, so the twin checks
    J4.2(b) shear rupture of the plate and of the support's face along a weld without returns. Manual
    Eq. 9-7, tmin = 6.19D/Fu, is that rupture for a plate with a fillet on each face, and Example
    II.A-11B applies it with the size its eccentric weld needs. Eq. 9-6, tmin = 3.09D/Fu, is the same
    for a support with the connection on one face, as II.A-15 applies it. So each check is the weld's
    strength times t/tmin, taken over the length the eccentric weld leaves effective, Rn/(0.60 FEXX
    Awe) (inferred). The checks have mode `weld_base_metal`. On the six gate weld brackets the plate
    reads 0.65 to 0.81 and the support 0.35 to 0.51 (measured). On `examples/steel/weld_bracket.petra`
    the 1/2 in. A36 plate is under the 0.533 in. that Eq. 9-7 asks of 5/16 in. fillets, so its rupture
    at the weld now governs at 0.413 (measured).
33. Linear weld groups loaded through their center. J2.4 and its User Note let a group whose
